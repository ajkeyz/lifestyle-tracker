UI UPGRADES (Make Each Question Feel Intentional)

1. Stronger Question Framing (Reduce Cognitive Noise)

Right now, the question appears almost immediately with options. It’s efficient—but a bit abrupt.

Upgrade
	•	Add a 1-line context buffer before the question:
“This is a moment where money, ego, and fear overlap.”

Then show the question.

Why
This primes reflection instead of impulse clicking.

⸻

2. Make the Timer Feel Supportive, Not Stressful

The timer bar + seconds can feel like pressure.

Upgrade
	•	Replace numeric countdown with:
	•	“Take your time” (first half)
	•	“Trust your instinct” (last 25%)
	•	Optional: fade animation instead of shrinking bar

Why
You’re training decision quality, not speed.

⸻

3. Answer Selection Feedback (Before Continue)

Currently, selecting an option highlights it, then “Continue”.

Upgrade
	•	After selection, add micro-affirmation text above Continue:
	•	“Interesting choice.”
	•	“Many people hesitate here.”
	•	“This is a common instinct.”

Not “correct” or “wrong”.

Why
This keeps it judgment-free while validating engagement.

⸻

4. Visual Weight of Options

All options look equally “valid,” which is good—but you can subtly guide reflection.

Upgrade
	•	Add micro-icons or tone cues:
	•	🟢 Practical
	•	🟡 Cautious
	•	🔵 Strategic
	•	🟠 Emotional

Don’t label them explicitly—let users infer.

⸻

5. Reduce Header Noise During the Question

The top bar (Q1 of 5, sun icon, etc.) competes slightly with the scenario.

Upgrade
	•	Fade header elements while answering
	•	Bring them back on transition

Why
During the question, nothing should be louder than the dilemma.

⸻

FEATURE UPGRADES (Where the Real Value Compounds)

1. Post-Question Reflection (The Gold Mine)

This is the biggest opportunity.

After each question (or at the end), show:
	•	Why people choose this
	•	What this choice optimizes for
	•	What it quietly trades off

Example for this scenario:

“Many people accept counteroffers for security.
Fewer consider how often the original reasons for leaving remain unchanged.”

No verdict. Just clarity.

⸻

2. Pattern Recognition Across Questions

Don’t score answers—connect them.

At the end of the 5-question drop:

“Today, you leaned toward short-term certainty over long-term change.”

Or:

“You consistently prioritized optionality.”

This turns 5 questions into insight.

⸻

3. Make “Ask for More Time” a Teachable Moment

Some answers (like B here) are pauses, not decisions.

Upgrade
	•	For “delay” answers, surface:
“Pausing can be wisdom—or avoidance. Context matters.”

This teaches nuance, which is rare and powerful.

⸻

4. Introduce Silent Counterfactuals

After answering, briefly show:

“Many people also considered: ‘What happens if nothing changes?’”

No extra buttons. No branching. Just mental simulation.
