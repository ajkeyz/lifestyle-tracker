Lifestyle Creep (the game) — how it works (the real experience)
What it is
Lifestyle Creep is a daily money game like Wordle, but instead of guessing a word you make real-life money decisions.
It’s designed to be:
    •    short (2–4 minutes/day)
    •    addictive (streaks + leaderboard + sharing)
    •    educational (but you don’t feel like you’re studying)
    •    competitive (rank vs friends)
The core concept it teaches is exactly what the name implies:
As your income grows, your spending “creeps” up unless you’re intentional — so the game trains you to build wealth without falling into lifestyle inflation traps.

What happens when you open the app
Home screen (every day)
You see:
    •    Lifestyle Creep — Daily Drop #__
    •    🔥 your streak
    •    🏆 your ranking in your Friend League
    •    button: PLAY TODAY’S DROP
It’s not a long session game — the entire point is:
“Quick daily decision challenge, like a ritual.”

The Daily Drop (the Wordle part)
Every day, everyone gets the same Daily Drop:
    •    5 scenarios
    •    each scenario is multiple choice (A/B/C/D)
    •    you have 15–25 seconds to decide (optional timer)
But the key: it’s not “finance trivia.”
It’s scenarios like:
    •    trip invites
    •    credit card debt
    •    layoffs
    •    bonuses
    •    rent upgrades
    •    scams
    •    investing temptations
It’s life.


Friend ranking (the social addiction)
After the drop you see:
    0.    Jade — Money Health 92
    0.    Sipho — Money Health 84
    0.    You — Money Health 78
    0.    Max — Money Health 63
So the game becomes:
“I’m beating you tomorrow.”
That’s what creates daily retention.


Example:
💻 Tech Girl/Guy Mode — “High Income, High Volatility”
Context: Cash $3,800 | Debt $200 | Credit 690 | Stress 44 | Portfolio: 70% in company stock
Q1: RSU concentration risk (real tech problem)
$10k RSUs vest. Your portfolio is already 70% company stock.
A) Hold all RSUs — belief = wealth
B) Sell 70% and diversify into index funds ✅
C) Sell 100% and go cash ✅ if layoff risk is high
D) Upgrade your apartment (fixed costs ↑)
Hard part: both B and C can be best depending on risk.

🛡️ Special Edition — “Fraud, Scams, Real-World Defense”
Context: Cash $1,500 | Debt $0 | Credit 700 | Stress 40
Q1: Caller ID spoof scam
Caller ID shows your bank. They know your name + last transaction. They ask for OTP.
A) Give OTP
B) Hang up and call number on back of card ✅
C) Ask them to email proof
D) Ask for employee ID then proceed
Hard part: A/D feel reasonable.
